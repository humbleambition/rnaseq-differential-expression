## 1. Question

Which genes respond to the synthetic glucocorticoid **dexamethasone** in
primary human airway smooth muscle (ASM) cells, and does the response
recover known glucocorticoid biology? Glucocorticoids are the mainstay
anti-inflammatory therapy in asthma, and ASM is one of their target
tissues.

## 2. Data

- **Study:** Himes BE et al. (2014) *RNA-Seq transcriptome profiling
  identifies CRISPLD2 as a glucocorticoid responsive gene that modulates
  cytokine function in airway smooth muscle cells.* PLoS ONE 9(6):
  e99625. <doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0099625>
- **GEO accession:**
  [GSE52778](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE52778),
  16 samples: four primary ASM donor cell lines, each untreated, 1 µM
  dexamethasone, 1 µM albuterol, or both (18 h).
- **Counts:** NCBI GEO RNA-seq pipeline raw gene counts (GRCh38.p13,
  NCBI Gene IDs), 16,802 genes tested after filtering in the primary
  comparison.
- **Annotation:** `org.Hs.eg.db` (Entrez ID to symbol, name and gene
  type).

The pattern is consistent with swapped or mis-annotated albuterol

does not try to resolve it. **The albuterol arms are excluded**, and the
primary comparison uses only the untreated and dexamethasone samples,
whose labels are internally consistent. These are the same eight samples
distributed in the Bioconductor `airway` package.

## 4. Differential expression: dexamethasone vs untreated

**Design:** `~ cell_line + treatment` (paired by donor), 8 samples.
Genes were kept with ≥ 10 counts in ≥ 4 samples. DESeq2 Wald test,
Benjamini-Hochberg FDR. Log2 fold changes were shrunk with the `normal`
prior for the MA plot and ranking. edgeR (quasi-likelihood F-test) and
limma-voom were fitted with the same design as cross-checks.

## 6. Interpretation

- Dexamethasone produces a large, highly reproducible transcriptional
  response in ASM cells: 4,743 genes at FDR \< 0.05, of which 1,071
  change more than two-fold, roughly balanced between induction and
  repression.
- The strongest hits recover canonical glucocorticoid-receptor targets:
  **FKBP5** (GR co-chaperone and negative-feedback regulator),
  **TSC22D3**/GILZ and **DUSP1**/MKP-1 (both key mediators of
  glucocorticoid anti-inflammatory action), **PER1**, **KLF15** and
  **ZBTB16**. **CRISPLD2**, the gene highlighted by the original study,
  is induced about 6.2-fold, which reproduces the published finding.
- Repressed genes include the adhesion molecule **VCAM1** and modest
  decreases in the inflammatory cytokine genes **IL6** (log2FC -0.81,
  FDR 0.015) and **CCL2** (log2FC -0.54, FDR 0.038), consistent with the
  anti-inflammatory profile of glucocorticoids. Up-regulated genes are
  enriched for extracellular-matrix organisation, cell-substrate
  adhesion and response to hormone stimulus, and down-regulated genes
  for GPCR signalling and cell-projection morphogenesis terms. These
  suggest effects on ASM structure and signalling beyond inflammation.
- DESeq2, edgeR and limma-voom agree closely (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.998 for
  fold changes; 4,628 genes significant in all three), so the
  conclusions do not depend on the choice of method.

## 7. Limitations

- n = 4 donors per group. The paired design controls donor effects, but
  inter-individual variation in response (e.g. N080611) is only partly
  captured.
- A single dose and time point (18 h). Primary vs secondary response
  genes cannot be separated.
- GO over-representation depends on the significance and fold-change
  cut-offs and on GO annotation bias; it is hypothesis-generating.
- The albuterol and combination arms were not analysed because of the
  metadata inconsistency described in Section 3.
